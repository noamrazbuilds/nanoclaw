### Risks/Unknowns

1.  **Deep Architectural Mismatches:** Hand-porting a feature may reveal it is fundamentally incompatible with a v2 assumption, requiring a full rewrite instead of a port.
2.  **Effort Escalation:** The 40-65 hour estimate is our best assessment, but a single complex, high-conflict task could significantly increase the time required.
3.  **Subtle Semantic Divergence:** The plan includes manual testing checkpoints to mitigate this, but subtle bugs where ported code runs but behaves incorrectly in edge cases may only be discovered post-cutover.
4.  **Tool Unreliability & AI Fallibility (META-RISK):** The process is mediated by AI assistants that have demonstrated fallibility (fabrication) and unreliability (outages). The plan's reliance on durable, human-auditable artifacts (`.patch` files, `.sh` scripts) is the primary mitigation.
5.  **Faded User Context:** The plan assumes the engineer has sufficient context on 7-month-old features to manually port them. Faded context may slow the process and increase the risk of implementation error.

### Unresolved Tensions

Even with a strong consensus, some genuine tensions remain where reasonable experts could disagree on the exact implementation. These represent trade-offs, not flaws.

*   **Rigor vs. Velocity:** The synthesized plan is heavily weighted towards safety, rigor, and verifiability. A different expert, facing a tighter deadline, might argue for a "fast path" that skips some verification for changes deemed "low risk," accepting a higher chance of failure for a potential speed-up. The current plan is conservative; the correct point on this spectrum depends on the user's risk tolerance vs. time constraints.

*   **Human vs. AI Agency:** The plan decisively demotes the AI Porter to a simple, deterministic executor and places all judgment work on the human engineer. This is the safest approach given the demonstrated AI fallibility. However, a more experimental approach could involve trying to equip the AI with more sophisticated rules for resolving certain classes of conflicts, aiming to automate more of the process. This represents a fundamental philosophical choice about the human-AI collaboration model in complex engineering tasks.
